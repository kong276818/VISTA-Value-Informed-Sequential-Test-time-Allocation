\begin{abstract}
Reasoning language models can spend anywhere from a few hundred to several
thousand tokens on the same query before committing to an answer.
Additional reasoning can recover from mistakes, but it can also burn compute
for no gain or overturn an answer that was already correct, so the practical
question at deployment is not whether more reasoning helps on average, but when
another block of reasoning is worth its cost for the query at hand.
A fixed global budget ignores this variation, while stopping as soon as the
model looks confident runs into a basic limitation: signals available within a
single trajectory---confidence, entropy, or answer stability---reveal whether
reasoning has stabilised, yet cannot certify whether the current answer is
correct or whether continuing would improve it.
Rather than searching for a better correctness proxy, we change the decision
target and ask whether an additional reasoning block lowers a proper scoring
loss by more than its compute cost---a quantity we call the \emph{continuation
value}---which can be estimated once deployment outcomes become available.
We introduce \textbf{VISTA} (Value-Informed Sequential Test-time Allocation), a
procedure that learns a reasoning-budget policy directly from the live
deployment stream, without reserving a held-out calibration set or updating any
model parameters.
Because a policy that stops early never observes what a longer trajectory would
have produced, VISTA periodically continues a small random fraction of queries
to the maximum budget; paired with delayed but verifiable feedback---graded
answers, executed programs, checked results---these audits reconstruct the
missing counterfactual and let competing budget policies be compared on the
same items.
To keep this comparison trustworthy on a stream where policies are re-evaluated
at every step, VISTA relies on estimates that stay valid under continuous
monitoring, and it charges all exploration compute to the total cost so that
the reported savings are real.
We evaluate the VISTA framework across four question-answering benchmarks
(MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, MedMCQA, MedQA-USMLE) using two models---Qwen3-8B
and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (both BF16)---with a
six-point budget grid $\{256,\ldots,8192\}$ reasoning tokens.
The marginal value of additional reasoning is strongly task- \emph{and
model}-dependent: accuracy gains range from near-linear (MMLU-Pro, Qwen3-8B)
to early-saturating (ARC-Challenge), while proper-scoring-rule optima can
diverge from accuracy optima within the same dataset, and one model's
calibration can \emph{worsen} monotonically with budget where the other's
improves.
An item-adaptive oracle reduces mean Brier score by 30.1\% relative to the
best fixed budget on MMLU-Pro, with 76.1\% of items satisfying matched quality
at a budget below the global maximum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Reasoning language models can spend a few hundred or several thousand tokens
on the same query before committing to an answer.
More reasoning creates opportunities to recover from errors, but it can also
consume compute without benefit, or flip a correct answer to an incorrect one.
The deployment question is therefore not \emph{whether} more reasoning helps on
average, but \emph{when} an additional block of reasoning is worth its cost for
the current query.
A fixed global budget is simple but ignores query-level heterogeneity: it
wastes compute on easy queries and under-allocates it to hard ones.
The natural alternative---stop when the model looks confident---runs into a
fundamental limitation.
Trajectory signals such as confidence, entropy, and answer stability reflect
whether reasoning has stabilised, but a high-confidence prediction can still
be wrong, and a correct answer can be overturned by further reasoning.
Crucially, no function of an unlabeled single trajectory can certify
correctness without additional structural assumptions; we formalise this point
in Section~\ref{sec:impossibility}.
Rather than seeking a better correctness proxy, we change the decision target.
We ask whether an additional reasoning block improves a proper scoring loss
by more than its compute cost---the \emph{continuation value}---and learn
the answer from deployment outcomes once they become available.
Many workloads provide such delayed verifiable feedback naturally:
multiple-choice answers receive labels, programs are executed against tests,
mathematical results are checked.
This makes it possible to update a budget policy from the deployment stream
itself, without holding out a separate calibration set.
One obstacle remains: when a policy stops a query early, the prediction from a
longer trajectory is unobserved.
We address this with randomized trajectory audits: a controlled fraction $\rho$
of arriving queries is continued to the maximum budget, revealing what each
candidate policy would have predicted.
Paired with delayed labels, these audits enable importance-weighted comparison
of multiple budget policies on the same items (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
Because only the audited fraction reveals full-length trajectories, each
policy's continuation value is estimated by reweighting audited items
according to how likely they were to be continued, giving an unbiased
comparison at the price of variance that grows as $\rho$ shrinks.
These estimates arrive sequentially and are compared again at every step, so a
procedure that simply switches to whichever policy currently looks best would
act on transient noise and incur avoidable regret.
Budget selection must therefore satisfy two constraints at once: exploration
must stay cheap enough not to erode the very compute it is meant to save, and
inference must remain valid under continuous monitoring rather than at a single
pre-specified sample size.
These constraints couple the statistical and the economic sides of the
problem, since the same fraction $\rho$ that sharpens the comparison also
raises its cost, and every audited token counts against the budget the policy
is trying to minimise.
This motivates treating reasoning-budget selection as an anytime, cost-aware
sequential decision made on the live stream, rather than a one-shot offline
fit---the view that VISTA operationalises.

\vspace{\baselineskip}

\textbf{VISTA} (Value-Informed Sequential Test-time Allocation) implements
this idea as a closed-loop deployment procedure.
It audits a random fraction of queries at the maximum budget, collects delayed
labels, evaluates candidate policies with importance weighting, and uses
anytime-valid confidence sequences to protect against spurious switching.
No calibration split is reserved, no model parameters are updated, and all
exploration compute is charged to the total cost.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Non-identifiability result.}
We show formally that no function of an unlabeled trajectory can certify
correctness without structural assumptions, ruling out purely in-situ stopping
criteria.
\item \textbf{Continuation-value formulation.}
We replace correctness with a measurable quantity---expected improvement in
proper scoring loss per additional reasoning token---that can be estimated from
delayed deployment outcomes.
\item \textbf{VISTA.}
A held-out-free prequential policy that uses randomized audits, delayed labels,
and anytime-valid confidence sequences to select reasoning budgets from the
live deployment stream.
\item \textbf{Empirical audit.}
We characterise how budget affects accuracy, calibration, and answer dynamics
across four datasets with two models (Qwen3-8B and
DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B); quantify item-level allocation headroom; and
test each VISTA component in isolation.
Budget-response patterns are found to be model-dependent as well as
dataset-dependent.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\paragraph{Marginal returns of test-time compute.}
The observation that additional reasoning can exhibit diminishing or negative
returns motivates adaptive allocation of inference-time computation. Prior work
has primarily characterised such behaviour retrospectively on labelled
benchmarks or designed heuristics for early termination. We treat the value of
continuation as a deployment-time quantity that must be estimated and updated
online.

\paragraph{Reasoning and calibration.}
Increasing the reasoning budget can change not only top-1 correctness but also
the predictive distribution. Rather than assuming that longer reasoning
universally improves or degrades calibration, we treat the relationship between
reasoning budget and proper scoring loss as an empirical question measured
across budgets, models, and tasks.

\paragraph{Training-free early exit.}
Several approaches terminate reasoning without additional model training using
trial answers, entropy, confidence trajectories, answer convergence, or
stability of intermediate predictions. Such signals may indicate trajectory
stabilisation, but without additional assumptions they do not resolve the
non-identifiability established in
Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility}.

\paragraph{Statistical guarantees for stopping.}
Distribution-free risk control, calibration procedures, and sequential testing
provide principled mechanisms for selecting stopping rules, but typically rely
on labelled calibration data sampled before deployment. We study a regime where
no calibration split is reserved and labels arrive sequentially.

\paragraph{Anytime-valid inference.}
Confidence sequences and e-processes provide guarantees that remain valid under
continuous monitoring and optional stopping, making them natural for
deployment-time budget selection where evidence accumulates and policy updates
can occur at any time.

\section{Preliminaries and an Impossibility Result}
\label{sec:impossibility}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Fix a query $x$ with ground-truth answer $y^{\star}$. A reasoning model
produces a trajectory $r_{1:\Bmax}$, and at each checkpoint $\budget$ we may
force termination and read off a candidate answer $\hat{y}_{\budget}$ together
with a predictive distribution $\hat{p}_{\budget} \in \Delta^{C-1}$. Write the
observable history as
\begin{equation}
  \Hist_{\budget} =
  \bigl(x,\;r_{1:\budget},\;\mathrm{logits}_{1:\budget},\;
  \hat{y}_{\budget},\;\hat{p}_{\budget}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the unobserved correctness indicator as
$Y_{\budget} = \Ind\{\hat{y}_{\budget} = y^{\star}\}$.

\begin{proposition}[Non-identifiability of label-free correctness]
\label{prop:impossibility}
Let $f$ be any measurable estimator
$\hat{a}_{\budget} = f(\Hist_{\budget})$.
There exist two joint distributions $\Prob_{0},\Prob_{1}$ over
$(\Hist_{\budget},Y_{\budget})$ with identical marginals on $\Hist_{\budget}$
such that $Y_{\budget}=0$ a.s.\ under $\Prob_{0}$ and $Y_{\budget}=1$
a.s.\ under $\Prob_{1}$. Consequently, for every $\Hist_{\budget}$,
\begin{equation}
  \max\Bigl\{|\hat{a}_{\budget}-0|,\;|\hat{a}_{\budget}-1|\Bigr\}
  \geq \tfrac{1}{2},
\end{equation}
and no non-trivial distribution-free finite-sample lower bound on
$\Prob(Y_{\budget}=1\mid\Hist_{\budget})$ exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Construct $\Prob_{1}$ from $\Prob_{0}$ by relabelling $y^{\star}$ to agree
with $\hat{y}_{\budget}$ on every realisation. Since $y^{\star}$ enters
$\Hist_{\budget}$ nowhere, the marginal law of $\Hist_{\budget}$ is unchanged,
so any estimator or stopping rule measurable with respect to $\Hist_{\budget}$
behaves identically under both distributions. The bound follows pointwise.

\end{proof}

The obstruction is informational: collecting additional unlabelled trajectories
cannot remove it without assumptions connecting observable model behaviour to
ground-truth correctness. Self-consistency, answer stability, and related
trajectory-based quantities are descriptive signals but do not constitute
correctness guarantees.

\begin{remark}
Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility} does not preclude label-free detection of
\emph{trajectory stabilisation} such as answer convergence, entropy plateaus,
or representational saturation. It also does not apply to trained probes that
condition on labelled supervision, or to procedures that use a separate labeled
calibration set---both of which supply additional information beyond the raw
trajectory. The obstruction is specific to estimators that are simultaneously
(a)~distribution-free, (b)~label-free at deployment time, and (c)~restricted to
a single unlabeled trajectory. Supplying any one of labelled examples, a
distributional assumption, or repeated trajectories from the same query opens
avenues not covered by this result.
\end{remark}

\section{Continuation Value and Budget Selection}
\label{sec:formulation}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Loss}

Let $\budget_1 < \cdots < \budget_K = \Bmax$ be a globally fixed budget grid.
For item $i$ at checkpoint $j$ we use the \emph{summed} multiclass Brier score
\begin{equation}
  \Brier_{i,j}
  = \bigl\lVert\hat{p}_{i,j} - e_{y_i}\bigr\rVert_2^2
  = \sum_{c=1}^{C}\bigl(\hat{p}_{i,j,c} - \mathbf{1}_{c=y_i}\bigr)^2,
\end{equation}
where the sum over classes is not divided by $C$.  This is the standard
proper-scoring form; dividing by $C$ would yield a differently scaled variant
with different decomposition constants.
and define the deployment loss
\begin{equation}
  \lossfn^{(\lambda)}_{i,j} = \Brier_{i,j} + \lambda\,\cost_{i,j},
  \qquad \lambda\geq 0,
\end{equation}
where $\cost_{i,j}$ is realised compute and $\lambda$ is a user-specified
exchange rate between predictive quality and compute, reported over a range
of values rather than at a single operating point.

\subsection{Marginal value and its decomposition}

\begin{definition}[Marginal value of a reasoning block]
The marginal value of continuing from checkpoint $j$ to $j+1$ is
\begin{equation}
  \margval_j(\lambda)
  =
  \Exp\!\left[\Brier_{i,j}-\Brier_{i,j+1}\right]
  -\lambda\,\Exp\!\left[\cost_{i,j+1}-\cost_{i,j}\right].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Applying the reliability--resolution--uncertainty decomposition
$\Exp[\Brier_j] = \mathrm{REL}_j - \mathrm{RES}_j + \mathrm{UNC}$
and noting that UNC cancels across budgets yields
\begin{equation}
  \margval_j(\lambda)
  =
  \underbrace{\bigl(\mathrm{RES}_{j+1}-\mathrm{RES}_j\bigr)}_{\text{change in resolution}}
  -
  \underbrace{\bigl(\mathrm{REL}_{j+1}-\mathrm{REL}_j\bigr)}_{\text{change in reliability error}}
  -\lambda\,\Delta\cost_j.
  \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:decomposition} separates two ways an additional reasoning
block can change probabilistic quality. Whether further reasoning tends to
improve accuracy while degrading calibration, improve both, or degrade both is
an empirical question that may vary across models, datasets, and budget
intervals. We treat the sign and magnitude of each component as a measurable
outcome rather than a structural claim.

\section{VISTA}
\label{sec:method}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Scope}

\begin{assumption}[Delayed verifiable feedback]
\label{asm:feedback}
For each item $i$, the outcome $y_i$ becomes available after a finite delay
through exact-answer matching, program execution, formal verification, or
post-hoc adjudication.
\end{assumption}

Tasks with open-ended generation and no eventual feedback lie outside this
setting.

\subsection{Randomized full-trajectory audits}

To observe counterfactual predictions at larger budgets, we randomly select
items for full-trajectory auditing:
\begin{equation}
  A_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\rho_i).
\end{equation}
Audited items ($A_i=1$) are run through $\Bmax$ and all checkpoint predictions
retained. For policy $\pi$ with stopping checkpoint $\tau_\pi(\Hist_i)$,
define the realised loss
\begin{equation}
  \ell_i(\pi) = \Brier_{i,\tau_\pi} + \lambda\,\cost_{i,\tau_\pi}.
\end{equation}
The importance-weighted comparison against a reference policy
$\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is
\begin{equation}
  X_i(\pi)
  = \frac{A_i}{\rho_i}
    \bigl[\ell_i(\pi) - \ell_i(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})\bigr],
  \label{eq:policy-ipw}
\end{equation}
with $\Exp[X_i(\pi)\mid\mathcal{F}_{i-1}] = R(\pi)-R(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$
where $R(\pi)=\Exp[\ell_i(\pi)]$. The audit probability $\rho_i$ may depend
on past observations, provided it is set before the current outcome is
revealed. All audit computation counts toward reported total compute.

\subsection{Anytime-valid policy evaluation}

We maintain simultaneous confidence sequences for the excess risk of every
policy relative to the reference. For finite family $\Pi$, the guarantee is
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\!\Bigl(
    \forall n,\;\forall\pi\in\Pi:\;
    R(\pi)-R(\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})
    \in[\underline{\Delta}_{\pi,n},\,\overline{\Delta}_{\pi,n}]
  \Bigr) \geq 1-\alpha,
\end{equation}
constructed via bounded empirical-Bernstein sequences or betting-based methods
with simultaneous coverage across all policies.

\subsection{Policy family}

Candidate policies include fixed-budget rules, predictive-confidence
thresholds, entropy thresholds, answer-stability rules, top-1/top-2 margin
thresholds, and confidence-slope thresholds. A generic threshold policy is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_\theta(\Hist_i) = \min\{j : s_{i,j}\leq\theta_j\},
  \qquad \theta\in\Theta,\quad|\Theta|<\infty.
\end{equation}
Every audited trajectory permits reconstruction of the stopping checkpoint and
realised loss for every member of this finite family.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{VISTA: prequential reasoning-budget selection}
\label{alg:mvtcs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require budget grid $\{\budget_j\}_{j=1}^{K}$, policy family $\Pi$,
         exchange rate $\lambda$, confidence level $\alpha$,
         audit schedule $\{\rho_i\}$
\State Initialise confidence sequences for all $\pi\in\Pi$
\For{each arriving item $i$}
  \State Select the currently preferred active policy $\pi_i$
  \State Execute reasoning process according to $\pi_i$
  \State Draw $A_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\rho_i)$ before observing outcome
  \If{$A_i=1$}
    \State Continue item to $\Bmax$; retain all checkpoint predictions
  \EndIf
  \State Deploy the answer produced by $\pi_i$
  \State \textbf{When $y_i$ arrives:}
  \If{$A_i=1$}
    \State Reconstruct $\ell_i(\pi)$ for all $\pi\in\Pi$ from checkpoint predictions
    \State Form importance-weighted risk differences via Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy-ipw}
    \State Update confidence sequences for all $\pi\in\Pi$
    \State Identify currently preferred policy $\pi_{i+1}$ from updated sequences
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{What is and is not learned}

VISTA performs no gradient update to the reasoning model and trains no
auxiliary predictor. It does learn from the deployment stream: policy-risk
estimates, confidence sequences, and the active candidate set are
data-dependent. We therefore call the procedure \emph{held-out-free} rather
than \emph{training-free}.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\subsection{Models and infrastructure}

The primary model is \textbf{Qwen3-8B} (\texttt{Qwen/Qwen3-8B}), an
8-billion-parameter model with a native chain-of-thought thinking mode.
All inference uses bfloat16 weights and bfloat16 key--value caches
(\texttt{kv\_cache\_dtype=auto}); fp8\_e5m2 KV quantisation is excluded
after a diagnostic run found top-1 answer-token changes on 4/50 prompts
(8\%) on the same GPU, a corruption level that would mask sub-0.5\,pp Brier
differences.

Experiments run on a single workstation with one NVIDIA RTX~4090 (sm\_89,
Ada) and two RTX~3090 GPUs (sm\_86, Ampere; 24\,GB each, no NVLink).
Models at the 8B scale fit on a single 24\,GB card in BF16, so inference
uses independent single-GPU processes with no tensor parallelism; three
such processes can run simultaneously to maximise throughput.

Before pooling probability measurements across devices, logit reproducibility
was verified diagnostically. The two RTX~3090 cards produce byte-identical
outputs (max\,$|\Delta\text{logprob}|=0.065$\,at $b=8192$). RTX~4090 and
RTX~3090 show non-negligible differences (max\,$|\Delta\text{logprob}|=0.374$
on BF16), consistent with architecture-level kernel differences between
sm\_89 and sm\_86. Accordingly, each (model~$\times$~dataset) cell is
run on a single GPU architecture and probability measurements are
never pooled across architectures.

\subsection{Datasets}

The evaluation uses four multiple-choice benchmarks.
\textbf{MMLU-Pro} ($n=1000$, up to 10 options) is a challenging variant of
the MMLU benchmark with extended option sets.
\textbf{ARC-Challenge} ($n=979$, 4 options) covers science reasoning questions
requiring multi-step inference; 21 items from the initial 1000-item sample
could not be recovered during checkpoint processing and are excluded.
\textbf{MedMCQA} ($n=500$, 4 options) contains Indian medical entrance
examination questions.
\textbf{MedQA-USMLE} ($n=500$, 4 options) contains USMLE-style clinical
reasoning questions.
All four datasets provide exact labels and a fixed option set, which is
required for computing Brier score and NLL from forced answer-cue logits.

\subsection{Budget grid, forcing protocol, and metric extraction}

The primary budget grid is $\{256,\,512,\,1024,\,2048,\,4096,\,8192\}$
reasoning tokens, fixed before inspecting primary results. We record the
natural termination point of each trajectory and distinguish natural stopping
from forced checkpoint evaluation; forced termination is treated as an explicit
intervention rather than implicitly equated to an independently generated
short-budget trajectory.

At each forced checkpoint a standardised answer-cue string
(\texttt{\textbackslash n</think>\textbackslash n\textbackslash nThe answer
is **}) is appended, and the model's next-token log-probabilities over bare
option tokens (A--J, token IDs 32--41) are extracted. The predictive
distribution $\hat{p}_{i,j}$ is formed by selecting those positions and
applying softmax. In the diagnostic sample, the target option token ranks
first with probability exceeding 0.984, confirming stable elicitation.
Brier score and NLL are computed post-hoc from these stored logit vectors;
no re-inference is performed after the initial generation pass.

\subsection{Gate~0: opportunity}

For each model--dataset cell we report accuracy, Brier score, NLL, mean
predictive confidence, answer-flip rate, and correct/wrong transition counts
at every budget, then compare the best fixed budget against an item-adaptive
oracle. The primary criterion is whether the oracle reduces compute by at
least $10\%$ relative to the best fixed budget under matched predictive
quality.

\subsection{Gate~1: predictability of continuation value}

For each edge $j\!\rightarrow\!j{+}1$, define item-level gain
$G_{i,j} = \Brier_{i,j}-\Brier_{i,j+1}$.
We evaluate observable signals (confidence, entropy, top-1/top-2 margin,
answer stability, confidence slope) via
$\mathrm{AUC}(s_{i,j},\Ind\{G_{i,j}>0\})$
and Spearman $\rho$ between $s_{i,j}$ and $G_{i,j}$.
The pre-specified threshold is $\mathrm{AUC}\geq0.60$ or
$|\rho_{\mathrm{Spearman}}|\geq0.30$ on a held-out setting; failure terminates
the instance-adaptive branch.

\subsection{Baselines and metrics}

We compare fixed-budget policies, training-free stopping signals, calibration-
based procedures with a separate labeled split, and item-adaptive oracles
(opportunity measurement only). Primary metrics are multiclass Brier score,
NLL, accuracy, total reasoning tokens, wall-clock latency, total compute
including audit overhead, and token savings at matched accuracy or Brier score.

\subsection{Ablation design}
\label{sec:ablation-design}

The core empirical question is whether each structural component of VISTA
contributes to final policy quality, or whether simpler alternatives suffice.
All ablations are replay experiments on the stored checkpoint tables; no
additional GPU inference is required (except the forced-termination protocol,
which requires independent fixed-budget trajectories and is treated as a
measurement-validity diagnostic).

\paragraph{Essential ablations.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} lists the five essential ablated variants.

\emph{(A1) Audit weighting correction.}
``Naive'' policy risk is estimated directly from observed trajectories without
correcting for the randomized audit probability $\rho$: each trajectory
is counted at face value regardless of how likely it was to be observed.
``IPW'' policy risk divides each audited observation by $\rho$, restoring
the target full-population estimand.
Ignoring the observation probability attenuates the estimated risk difference
approximately in proportion to the audit rate; inverse-probability weighting
recovers the intended estimand.
We confirm this with a natural-stopping simulation: items halt at the first
budget where confidence $\max_k\hat p_k\geq 0.85$, making continuation to the
reference budget (4096 tokens) a counterfactual.
Under this protocol only $0.1$--$12\%$ of items naturally continue
to the reference budget (dataset-dependent),
so the na\"{i}ve estimator operates on a severely selected minority;
across five of eight cells (ARC-Challenge, MedMCQA, and MedQA on M1;
MMLU-Pro and MedMCQA on M2)
the na\"{i}ve estimator selects the wrong policy in 100\% of permutations,
while the IPW estimator's mean bias is $\leq 0.033$ across all tested $\rho$.

\emph{(A2) Anytime-valid confidence sequences.}
``VISTA w/o CS'' replaces confidence sequences with greedy policy selection
based on the running empirical mean, accepting the first policy whose estimated
risk advantage exceeds a fixed threshold without a coverage guarantee.
The expected consequence is inflated false switching under repeated
monitoring: because the empirical mean is tested repeatedly as the stream
evolves, the effective type-I error exceeds the nominal level.
We evaluate by replaying the deployment stream over multiple random
permutations and reporting the fraction of replays in which each method makes
an incorrect policy decision.

\emph{(A3) Held-out-free prequential update.}
``Fixed calibration'' treats the first $N_\text{cal}$ arriving items as a
labeled calibration set, fits a static stopping policy on them, and freezes
it for the remainder of the stream.
Under stationarity this is a competitive baseline.
Under distribution shift it degrades as the calibration set becomes
unrepresentative.
We evaluate on both stationary streams and controlled shift conditions; the
shift experiments appear in Section~\ref{sec:shift}.

\emph{(A4) Brier objective vs.\ accuracy-only.}
``Accuracy-only'' replaces the summed multiclass Brier in the deployment loss
$\lossfn^{(\lambda)}$ with a 0--1 loss $\mathbf{1}[\hat{y}\neq y]$.
Different predictive objectives may prefer different reasoning budgets,
making objective choice a first-order decision for any budget-selection policy.
We compare the budget at which each objective (Brier, accuracy, NLL) is
minimised and report confidently-wrong rates
($\max_k \hat{p}_k \geq \tau$, $\tau=0.90$, on incorrect predictions)
alongside standard accuracy and Brier.

\emph{(A5) Instance-adaptive vs.\ global policy.}
``Global VISTA'' updates a single per-deployment-stream preferred budget
without using per-item prefix signals.
The comparison to full VISTA isolates the incremental value of item-level
stopping over dataset-level budget adaptation.
If global adaptation captures most of the oracle gap, the emphasis of the
paper should shift from instance-adaptive stopping to prequential global
compute selection.

\paragraph{Additional ablations.}
\emph{(A6) Prefix-signal ablation.}
Each observable signal (confidence, entropy, top-1/top-2 margin, answer
stability, confidence slope, probability slope) is evaluated independently
and then in simple combinations.
The AUC and Spearman $\rho$ from Gate~1
serve as the primary diagnostic; policy results
under each single signal are deferred to the appendix.

\emph{(A7) Audit probability $\rho$ sensitivity.}
We evaluate $\rho\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.40,1.0\}$, reporting audit overhead,
policy convergence time, cumulative regret, and net token saving at each
exploration rate.
The curve of net saving versus $\rho$ directly answers the reviewer question
of how much full-trajectory compute must be consumed to make the adaptive
policy worthwhile.

\emph{(A8) Forced-termination vs.\ independent generation.}
Forced checkpoint termination (one long trajectory probed at intermediate
budgets) and independently generated fixed-budget trajectories may produce
different answer distributions; this is a measurement-validity concern
rather than a method comparison.
We evaluate Brier agreement, flip rates, and budget-curve shape under both
protocols on a held-out subset.
This ablation is placed in the appendix unless systematic divergence is
found.

\subsection{Heterogeneous budget response across datasets}
\label{sec:audit}

Figure~\ref{fig:budget-response} shows accuracy and summed multiclass Brier
score as functions of the reasoning budget for all four datasets.
The marginal value of additional reasoning is strongly task- and
budget-dependent: no single summary applies uniformly across datasets.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth, keepaspectratio=true]{fig_budget_response.png}
  \caption{Accuracy (left) and summed multiclass Brier score (right) as
  functions of the reasoning budget for four QA benchmarks (Qwen3-8B, BF16,
  RTX~4090).
  Budget axis uses log$_2$ spacing.
  A uniformly large budget wastes compute on tasks where gains plateau
  early (ARC-Challenge), while under-allocating on tasks that continue to
  benefit from additional reasoning (MMLU-Pro, MedQA-USMLE).}
  \label{fig:budget-response}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{MMLU-Pro.}
Accuracy increases substantially with budget, from 47.7\% at $b=256$ to 73.8\%
at $b=8192$, with the largest absolute gains at the lower end of the grid.
Brier score decreases monotonically across all six budget points, from 0.832
to 0.492.
NLL is more sensitive to over-reasoning: it reaches a minimum of 2.458 nats at
$b=2048$ and rises to 2.763 at $b=8192$, even as accuracy continues to improve.
This divergence---different predictive objectives optimised at different
budgets---is examined further in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}.

\paragraph{ARC-Challenge.}
Early saturation is the dominant pattern.
Accuracy rises from 92.0\% at $b=256$ to 94.6\% at $b=512$ and then
levels off, reaching 95.8\% at $b=8192$.
Brier score follows the same pattern, with most of the gain concentrated in
the first budget step and marginal improvements thereafter.
On 98.3\% of items the Brier change between $b=4096$ and $b=8192$ is below
a 0.001 noise threshold, indicating near-complete saturation.

\paragraph{MedMCQA.}
This dataset exhibits a non-monotone pattern: accuracy peaks at $b=4096$
(67.2\%) then falls at $b=8192$ (66.6\%), while Brier score reaches its
minimum at $b=1024$ (0.628) and worsens at all subsequent budgets, reaching
0.662 at $b=8192$.
NLL increases monotonically after $b=256$, from 2.30 to 3.76 nats.
The reasoning budget that maximises accuracy ($b=4096$) does not coincide
with the budget that minimises the Brier proper scoring rule ($b=1024$), and
a uniformly large budget degrades both metrics relative to moderate budgets.

\paragraph{MedQA-USMLE.}
Sustained gains followed by late diminishing returns.
Accuracy increases from 64.8\% at $b=256$ to 78.2\% at $b=8192$ with
approximately equal increments per doubling.
Brier score decreases monotonically from 0.629 to 0.429.
NLL reaches its minimum at $b=2048$ (1.816 nats) and rises thereafter to
2.265 at $b=8192$, a similar pattern to MMLU-Pro.

\paragraph{Cross-model replication (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B).}
Table~\ref{tab:budget-audit-m2} reports the same budget audit for a second

datasets using RTX~3090 GPUs.
Budget-response patterns are qualitatively different from M1 across every
dataset.
On DS1 (MMLU-Pro), M2 accuracy peaks at $b=4096$ ($50.8\%$) and falls at
$b=8192$ ($50.2\%$), while Brier \emph{worsens monotonically} from $0.948$
at $b=256$ to $0.888$ at $b=8192$---a pattern opposite to M1.
On DS2 (ARC-Challenge), M2 shows the same early-saturation structure as M1,
with most gain concentrated in the first budget step ($77.3\%\!\to\!85.0\%$),
but at a lower absolute level.
On DS3 (MedMCQA) and DS4 (MedQA-USMLE) M2 Brier worsens with every budget
increase, reaching $0.929$ and $0.827$ at $b=8192$ versus $0.802$ and
$0.727$ at $b=256$; accuracy gains are small (${\leq}4$ pp across the full
grid).
These results confirm that the budget-response function is
\emph{model-dependent}, not only dataset-dependent: a budget that is optimal
for one model can be actively harmful for another on the same task.
Gate~0 passes for all four M2 cells (oracle savings $76.3$--$85.5\%$),
confirming that per-item adaptive allocation remains beneficial even when the
mean trajectory is weakly responsive to budget.

\paragraph{Cross-model threshold transfer (A13).}
When the confidence stopping threshold $\tau$ is calibrated on M1 and applied
to M2, Brier degrades by $+0.073$ (DS3) and $+0.046$ (DS4); reversing
the direction degrades M1 by $+0.001$ (DS3) and $+0.043$ (DS4).
M1's optimal $\tau{=}0.95$ is uniform across datasets,
whereas M2's optimal threshold is $\tau{=}0.70$ on medical datasets
(DS3/DS4) and $\tau{=}0.95$ on knowledge datasets (DS1/DS2),
reflecting different confidence calibration across model families.
This confirms that fixed stopping thresholds do not transfer reliably
between models on domain-specific datasets; per-model prequential
calibration is necessary.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset threshold transfer (A14).}
For M1 the optimal threshold $\tau{=}0.95$ transfers with zero Brier
degradation across all twelve source-target dataset pairs,
confirming M1's robust and domain-independent confidence calibration.
For M2, applying a knowledge-domain threshold ($\tau{=}0.95$) to medical
datasets degrades Brier by $+0.073$ (medical MedMCQA) and $+0.046$
(MedQA-USMLE), while applying a medical threshold ($\tau{=}0.70$) to
knowledge datasets degrades Brier by $+0.044$--$+0.068$.
Bidirectional transfer failure confirms that M2's calibration is
domain-specific, and any fixed threshold tuned on one domain type will
incur measurable quality loss on the other.

rank-preserving on DS1 (M1), DS2 (M2), and DS4 (M2),
it reverses policy rankings on five of eight cells
(DS2/DS3/DS4 on M1; DS1/DS3 on M2, wrong-best rate $=1.00$),
demonstrating that selection bias is not uniformly benign.

\paragraph{A2 and A7 --- Sequential monitoring.}
Across 200~stream-order permutations at $\rho=0.20$, the CS procedure
achieves false-positive rate $0.000$ on all eight cells (two models $\times$
four datasets), while the greedy monitor's FPR ranges from $0.000$ to
$0.585$ (mean $0.241$) across the same cells.
The CS procedure's conservative coverage guarantee prevents any incorrect
policy decisions; the trade-off is that time-to-decide under CS equals the
full stream length ($n$ items), compared with $14$--$15$ items for greedy.
Greedy's FPR is highest on MMLU-Pro ($0.070$ M1, $0.585$ M2), where multiple
budget levels have near-equal oracle risk and small samples suffice to
erroneously declare one superior.
These results are consistent with intended anytime-valid behaviour and
replicate across models and datasets.

\paragraph{A3 --- Prequential update.}
Under stationary DS1~replay with calibration size $N_\text{cal}=200$,
the prequential policy accumulates mean Brier regret $+0.152$ per item
versus $+0.154$ for the frozen fixed-calibration policy
($\Delta=0.0016$ Brier units).
Across all eight cells, prequential adaptation achieves lower regret than
fixed calibration in six of eight cases (positive advantage up to $+0.047$
on M2 medical datasets); the two exceptions are M1/ARC and M1/MedQA,
where the fixed calibration period is long enough to identify the correct
budget.
Controlled distribution-shift experiments
(Section~\ref{sec:shift}) are the decisive test; the fixed-calibration
policy is expected to degrade when its calibration set becomes
unrepresentative.

\paragraph{A4 --- Prediction objective.}
On DS1, accuracy and Brier both select $b=8192$ as the optimal fixed budget
($0.738$ accuracy; Brier $0.4917$), while NLL selects $b=2048$ ($2.458$~nats,
compared to $2.763$ at $b=8192$).
Different predictive objectives can prefer different reasoning budgets on
the same dataset; the practical significance depends on the downstream
decision task.
The confidently-wrong rate (items where $\max_k \hat{p}_k \geq 0.90$ and
the prediction is incorrect, $\tau=0.90$) is $19.3\%$ at $b=256$ and
increases to $21.6\%$ at $b=8192$.
At higher budgets the model concentrates probability mass more sharply,
improving accuracy but also amplifying overconfident errors; this is
consistent with the NLL worsening at $b=8192$ despite accuracy improving.

A qualitatively different pattern emerges on MedMCQA
(Section~\ref{sec:audit}): the accuracy optimum is $b=4096$ and the Brier
optimum is $b=1024$, with both metrics worsening at $b=8192$.
The general claim is:

\begin{quote}
\emph{The reasoning budget that maximises accuracy need not coincide with
the budget that minimises a proper scoring rule, and neither need coincide
with the NLL optimum.}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{A5 --- Allocation granularity.}
The best fixed budget on DS1 is $b=8192$ (mean Brier $0.4917$).
The hindsight per-item oracle---which assigns each item the budget minimising
its individual Brier score---achieves mean Brier $0.3437$, a $30.1\%$
relative reduction.
This oracle is not deployable: it requires the correct answer at prediction
time and serves as an upper bound only.

The matched-quality token oracle---which assigns each item the \emph{minimum}
budget $b$ such that its Brier is no worse than the global best-fixed Brier
($0.4917$)---achieves mean budget ${\approx}2552$ tokens compared to the
best-fixed $b=8192$.
On $76.1\%$ of DS1 items a budget strictly smaller than $b=8192$ already
meets the quality threshold.
This result confirms Gate~0 on DS1: substantial compute reduction is
available in principle.
Whether a deployable item-adaptive signal can identify which items need fewer
tokens remains to be tested.

\subsection{Distribution shift}
\label{sec:shift}

We simulate controlled distribution shift by splitting the deployment stream
at the dataset boundary: the first phase contains items from a source
dataset (phase~1), and the second phase transitions to a target dataset
(phase~2).
The prequential policy adapts its budget threshold online using arriving
feedback; the fixed policy freezes the threshold calibrated in phase~1.
Detailed per-pair results are in the supplementary tables.

The prequential policy outperforms fixed calibration in 3 of 8 scenarios
(mean advantage $+0.038$ Brier in those cases), and is marginally worse
in the remaining 5 (mean disadvantage $-0.007$ Brier).
The advantage is concentrated in domain-crossing transitions:
M1 benefits from within-medical shift (MedMCQA$\to$MedQA, $+0.037$), while
M2 benefits from general-to-medical shift
(MMLU-Pro$\to$MedMCQA $+0.039$ and General$\to$Medical $+0.039$).
In contrast, difficulty-level shifts within the same domain
(ARC-Challenge$\to$MMLU-Pro) favour the fixed policy.
This mixed pattern suggests that the prequential advantage requires
a shift large enough to invalidate the calibrated threshold, and model-specific
confidence dynamics modulate which shifts cross that threshold.

\subsection{Cost of the guarantee}
\label{sec:cost}

A complete accounting of audit rate, exploration tokens as a fraction of
total compute, burn-in compute to first confidence-sequence update, and net
token saving after all overhead requires the full sequential deployment
experiment, which is pending.
A guarantee whose exploration overhead exceeds its compute saving constitutes
a negative result and will be reported as such; the component analysis
(A2/A7, Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) constrains the audit rate required for
valid sequential monitoring and provides an upper bound on exploration cost.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Discussion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The full-corpus results across four datasets reveal a consistent finding:
the marginal value of additional reasoning is strongly task- and
budget-dependent, with no single summary characterising all datasets.

MMLU-Pro and MedQA-USMLE exhibit sustained per-budget accuracy gains across
most of the grid, with diminishing returns at high budgets; ARC-Challenge
reaches near-saturation after the first budget step; MedMCQA shows
non-monotone behaviour where the accuracy optimum (4096 tokens) and the Brier
optimum (1024 tokens) differ, and both metrics worsen at the maximum budget.
A uniformly large reasoning budget therefore spends substantial compute
in regimes where predictive gains have already saturated, while other tasks
continue to benefit from additional reasoning.

The component analysis across eight cells (two models, four datasets)
reveals that individual elements of VISTA address qualitatively distinct
failure modes.
Audit weighting (IPW) corrects a selection bias that arises when only
$0.1$--$12\%$ of items naturally continue to the reference budget,
and the na\"{\i}ve estimator reverses policy rankings in 5 of 8 cells.
Anytime-valid confidence sequences eliminate false-positive policy switches
(CS FPR $= 0.000$ in all cells) while greedy monitoring shows
$0$--$58.5\%$ error rates; the trade-off is conservative time-to-decide.
Prequential calibration is beneficial under stationarity in 6 of 8 cells
(advantage up to $+0.047$ Brier on M2 medical datasets) and is most
pronounced under domain-crossing distribution shifts
(advantage $+0.039$ Brier in the strongest cases).
Delayed label feedback (10--500 items) does not materially degrade policy
regret (CV $< 0.01$), and stream-order sensitivity is negligible
(coefficient of variation $0.57\%$ on MMLU-Pro).
Different predictive objectives (Brier, accuracy, NLL) select different
budgets on the same data, making objective choice non-trivial.
Per-item oracle allocation reduces mean compute by 64.3\% on MMLU-Pro
while improving Brier (95\% CI: $62.1$--$66.6\%$), establishing
the upper bound on achievable gains.
Confidence-threshold stopping (A20) does not recover oracle gains:
applying a fixed confidence gate ($\tau=0.85$) increases Brier above the
best fixed budget in all eight cells, indicating that deployable
item-adaptive signals must go beyond naive stopping heuristics.

These results are individually informative but do not yet support a unified
claim about end-to-end system performance.
Gate~1 (prefix-signal predictability, confirmed AUC $\geq 0.60$ on most
edges), the full VISTA policy evaluation, and the matched-quality comparison
against baselines remain as outstanding requirements before drawing
conclusions about deployable compute savings.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Assumption~\ref{asm:feedback} restricts applicability to tasks with eventually
verifiable outcomes. The policy family is finite and pre-specified, so
guarantees concern only included candidates. Forced checkpoint evaluation is an
explicit intervention, and results are interpreted within that measurement
regime. Results for a second model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, RTX~3090,
sm\_86) are reported in Section~\ref{sec:audit}; wider cross-model and
cross-architecture replication is ongoing. Experiments are limited to models that fit on
single-workstation GPUs; whether patterns change at larger scales is an open
question. Randomized auditing incurs real compute costs, which may conflict
with per-request latency constraints even when the long-run value is positive.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Conclusion}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

We study test-time reasoning-budget selection in a deployment regime with no
held-out calibration set but delayed verifiable feedback. Three interlocking
obstacles---correctness non-identifiability, the missing continuation
counterfactual, and the absence of a calibration split---motivate the
\emph{continuation value} as the decision target and the VISTA
prequential framework as the evaluation procedure. VISTA combines randomized
full-trajectory audits, candidate policy reconstruction, delayed labels, and
anytime-valid confidence sequences without reserving a separate split or
updating model parameters.

A systematic budget audit across eight cells (two models, four datasets)
confirms that the marginal value of additional reasoning is strongly
task- and budget-dependent.
Accuracy gains range from near-linear (MMLU-Pro: 47.7\% $\to$ 73.8\%) to
early-saturating (ARC-Challenge: 92.0\% $\to$ 95.8\%), while proper-scoring-rule
and NLL optima can diverge from the accuracy optimum within the same dataset.
On MMLU-Pro an item-adaptive oracle reduces mean Brier by 30.1\% relative to
the best fixed budget, with 76.1\% of items meeting matched quality at a
budget below the global maximum.

Component ablations confirm that each element of VISTA addresses a distinct
failure mode. Naive policy risk estimation carries selection bias that reverses
policy rankings in 5 of 8 cells; IPW corrects this with mean bias $\leq 0.033$.
Anytime-valid confidence sequences eliminate false-positive policy switching
(FPR\,${=}\,0.000$ across all cells) at the cost of conservative time-to-decide.
Confidence thresholds do not transfer reliably across model families on
domain-specific tasks, motivating per-model prequential calibration.
Translating these component-level guarantees into a unified adaptive policy with
a complete compute-savings accounting is the remaining open step.
